# Check answers before you share

Allow 4 minutes. A hallucination is a convincing output that is wrong. A citation is a starting point for checking, not a guarantee.

NZ Digital Government's guidance is written for the public service. Its advice to verify outputs and keep human oversight is also useful for a small business. It does not impose a new private-sector rule by itself.

## Use a five-point check

1. Open the cited file or page. Is it the right source and current?
2. Check names, dates, figures and arithmetic against the source.
3. Separate decisions from proposals. Look for omitted qualifications.
4. Confirm the reader may see the information.
5. Have the responsible person approve the final output.

When a source is missing or a claim matters, stop and find reliable evidence. Do not ask Copilot to create a citation just to make the answer look complete. A request to use only supplied notes can help, but it cannot guarantee accuracy.

## Try it: 2 minutes

These invented source notes say: "Trial the checklist. Draft due Tuesday next week. Review date not agreed. Buddy programme discussed."

A flawed illustrative summary says: "The team approved the checklist and buddy programme. The manager will complete the review on Wednesday next week."

Find two unsupported claims. Then rewrite the summary with a separate open question.

Check: the buddy programme was not approved. No review date was agreed. An open question to ask is, "When will the manager review the draft?"

Done when: you can correct the meaning using the source, without asking AI to grade itself.
